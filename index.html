A research platform for AI in education

An AI tutor you can take apart, on a platform built to run the study.

Hoot is two tutoring functions that switch on separately, and the study infrastructure around them: assignment, logging, consent and export. It is for researchers, faculty and instructors who want to know which part of an AI tutor does what.

The problem

Performance is not learning

48% better

on practice problems, with a plain chatbot.

17% worse

on the exam, once it was removed.

That is one large study of students given a plain chatbot (Bastani et al., 2025, PNAS). Doing well while the tool is open is not the same as having learned (Soderstrom & Bjork, 2015).

The effect depends on how the AI is designed, not on whether it is present.

The gap

What current studies can't tell you

AI or no AI
Most studies compare a class with AI to a class without. They can't say which part helped.
One lump
Designed tutors are tested whole. If the result is good or bad, the cause stays inside the box.
Rebuilt every time
Each research team builds randomization, logging, consent and data cleaning from scratch.
Two functions

Two functions, switched on separately

Each can run alone or together, so a study can test one against the other.

Answers from the course. Hoot answers only from the material the instructor uploaded, cites where each answer came from, and declines anything outside it.

The student teaches. The student explains how to solve a problem, in their own words, to an owl that is competent but clueless. The owl asks about exactly the part that was thin or wrong. Nothing in Apollo affects course grades.

Why the responses can be trusted

Three rules

The judge never writes
The judge is a classifier. For each step of an authored solution map it chooses from a fixed set of labels.
What's covered stays covered
Labels add up by a fixed rule, and each credited step points to the turn that earned it.
The persona never sees the answer
The owl asks its questions without access to the solution.

Built for research

Four things a study needs

Study design
Arms, access gating and stratified assignment are settings.
Causal checks
The seed and assignments are logged, interaction logs are stamped by arm, and usage is reported by arm.
Consistent treatment
Every student in an arm gets the same intervention.
Participant safeguards, enforced in software
Consent is collected in the platform and hidden from the instructor until grades post. Study IDs are random. No course grades are stored. Small groups are suppressed in exports. Under-18s and withdrawals are excluded automatically.

The platform enforces a study's safeguards. A review board still approves each protocol.
