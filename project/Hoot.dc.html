What it is

A teaching layer the institution owns

Hoot is delivered institutionally, grounded only in the professor's released course materials, citation-locked, and owned by the school. It authenticates students, checks course membership, and answers only from what the instructor has released.

  • Not a generic student chatbot.
  • Not a personal notebook.
  • Not shadow AI outside school governance.
Governance

Shadow AI today vs Hoot

Shadow AI today

Private. Uneven. Unmonitored. Outside institutional control.

Students paste coursework into tools no one governs. Some have access, some don't. The institution can't see or shape any of it.

Hoot

Course-scoped. Cited. Membership-gated. Institution-owned.

Every answer is built only from released materials and stamped with a citation, available to each enrolled student on equal footing, hosted by the school.

How it works

Two halves, one system

The answering half. A student's question becomes a cited answer built only from released course materials. If nothing relevant survives retrieval, Hoot says so instead of guessing — it can't point to a page it never read.

Authenticate → Retrieve → Rank → Ground → Cite

Only released materials are searchable. Weekly slides and notes unlock as the course reaches that week; textbooks stay visible throughout.

The teaching half. A teach-back partner. The student explains a concept back, and Apollo grades what they explain against the teacher's own worked solution. It structurally can't hand over the answer — it can only check yours.

Grading is structural, not a single score: coverage, soundness, and structural similarity, plus a misconception safety layer so fluent-but-wrong explanations don't pass.
For institutions

From one student to the whole classroom

Every graded answer updates a persistent, interpretable belief for each student and concept — confused, shaky, or mastered — that aggregates into a classroom map: class-wide strengths, weak concepts, common misconceptions, and the students who are stuck.

Pilot → Measure → Expand

Pilot one to three courses at low or no cost against success metrics agreed in advance — TA hours deflected, questions resolved. Expand to a departmental subscription once they're met.
